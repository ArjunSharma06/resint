# --- PubMed Central, screened for inline NHST: 30 ----------------
# NOT picked by topic. stats/grim, stats/pvalue-mismatch and
# stats/significance-unsupported have never fired on a real paper, and
# three explanations fit equally well: the rules are broken, the
# extraction is broken, or the corpus held no inline NHST. Picking by
# subject cannot separate them -- 'psychology journal' is not the same
# property as 'reports t and p in running text'.
#
# Screened with tools/screen_nhst.py on raw text, deliberately NOT with
# parse/extract.py: selecting with our own extractor would choose the
# papers it already handles, and any fire rate measured afterwards would
# be inflated by construction.
#
#   statistic:  t(20)  F(2,45)  r(48)  z=1.96  t=2.086  chi2  chi-square  X2
#   p-value:    p = .03   p<0.001   P > .05   (XML entities decoded first,
#               or every escaped 'p &lt; .001' is invisible -- that bug cost
#               half the yield before it was found)
#   a pair must fall within 200 characters, and 2 pairs are required, so a
#   lone methods-section 'alpha = .05' does not qualify
#
# Kept in name order, NOT ranked by hit count: taking the densest N would
# build a corpus of the most statistics-heavy papers in medicine, and any
# rate measured on it would then read as a rate on papers generally.
#
# Pool: listed 2026-09-01 from five MeSH searches -- psychology,
# clinical-trial, epidemiology, neuroscience, public-health -- 289 listed,
# 222 available in the open-access subset. Of those 222, 30 carry inline
# NHST and 192 do not. Hits among the kept ranged 2 to 59, median 5.
# 9 of the 30 were already cached from a 2026-08-27 pull.
#
# THIS STRATUM IS SMALLER THAN INTENDED. 100 was the target; 30 is what an
# honestly drawn pool yields at a 14% pass rate. It was not topped up by
# trawling a larger pool, because a stratum drawn from 700 articles is not
# comparable to one drawn from 222 and the cross-stratum reading would
# then be meaningless. If the statistics rules fire on 0 of 30, that is the
# result. notes/sweep-log.md records the ceiling that makes a low count
# expected, and the table-extraction gap that makes it a floor.
